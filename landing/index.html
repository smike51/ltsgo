Put a book on the map of time

Every travel book is a journey that really happened somewhere, at some time. We lay the text on a map of its own era, join it with an encyclopedia and a library — and the journey comes alive in time and space.

Phileas Fogg’s route, 2 October – 21 December 1872

How it works

Four layers, one journey

  1. I

    The book

    The full text, chapter by chapter. Every stop, date and person is anchored to a verbatim quote.

  2. II

    The map of time

    Borders, city names and roads as they were in that year. Königsberg, not Kaliningrad; Edo, not Tokyo.

  3. III

    The encyclopedia

    Who the people really were, checked against encyclopedias in several languages. Where they disagree, we show both.

  4. IV

    The library

    Engravings, early photographs, postcards and maps of the period — each with its author and licence.
